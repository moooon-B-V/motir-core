Approvals

Which decisions motir holds work for, and who is asked to make them. Turning one on is what makes Motir stop and ask.

Acceptance video approval

When a story’s end-to-end run produces a video receipt, hold the story and ask a person to approve it. The run records the video either way — this decides whether anyone is asked to sign it off.

The state line carries a NAME and a CONSEQUENCE, because “On” alone does not tell a reader what their project just started doing.

2 · OFF. Two consequences, and both are stated: nobody is asked, and nothing is uploaded. A project with the gate off raises no acceptance decision and shows no panel on its stories.

This corrects a defect in the shipped control, found by rendering it. Here the entitlement decides BOTH. MOTIR-4925 owes this; design-notes.md records it.

4 · Read-only — a member who may browse the project but does not hold workflow:manage. The state is fully legible and the control is inert, with a line saying why. The room is NOT hidden: what a project asks of you is something you are entitled to know.

5 · Why a ROOM, and not a row in one that already exists — the argument, drawn so it is reviewable beside the thing it justifies. Decided from the switch’s readers and writers, grepped on origin/main 2026-09-11.
Who READS the flag
  • acceptanceVideoEligibilityService — the one computation, and the only direct reader
  • …consumed by AcceptancePanel (the item page), publishAuth (the CI publish gate) and publishAcceptanceResult (the MCP publish gate)
  • …and, once MOTIR-4950 registers the kind, the acceptance_result gate’s own creation path
Who WRITES it
  • this control
  • the acceptance panel’s own Turn on action, whose settingsHref and #acceptance-video anchor follow the switch here

Nothing in planning, board, estimation, fields or components touches it, in either direction.

Rejected, and why
  • Automation › AI planning — the first pass’s answer, now withdrawn. Nothing in the planning path reads the flag; the room configures the PLANNER, and its ai:configure gate would make “may this project ask for acceptance approval” depend on “may this person configure the planner”.
  • Work › Estimation — chosen by resemblance to a small project-scoped toggle. Estimation reads nothing here.
  • A row inside Work › Workflow — the closest call. Rejected because the workflow room is about the status GRAPH, and a gate is a decision a PERSON makes that the graph then obeys; folding one into the other loses the distinction the epic exists to draw. The two stay neighbours instead.
The verdict. What those consumers add up to is an approval gate — the concept epic MOTIR-4878 exists for — so the switch belongs with the gates and not with whatever surface it resembles. And it is a ROOM rather than a single card because a second project-tier gate switch is already decided: docs/decisions/approval-gates.md §7 moves Workspace.subtaskPrMergeMode to Project.prMergeMode, where manual RAISES a pull_request_merge gate and auto raises none — the same sentence this card’s switch says, about a different kind. §7 also asks for “a deep link from the approval surface… to the switch that stops asking them”, and that link needs somewhere to land.

What the room holds on the day MOTIR-4925 ships: one card. That is drawn honestly in panels 1–4 and nothing here draws the merge-mode row, which is MOTIR-4882’s to design and build not drawn here. The room is justified by the readers and writers above, not by a second card this asset would be inventing.
