Where a project admin claims the workspace's subdomain and connects a domain they own — Story MOTIR-3878, subtask MOTIR-4211. Every address renders motir.site as the base; the string is configuration (MOTIR_PUBLIC_TENANT_DOMAIN), bought by MOTIR-4208 and set by MOTIR-4214, so read it as <base>. The behaviour of every control is specified by MOTIR-4215, MOTIR-4216, MOTIR-4219 and MOTIR-4228 and by the public-tenant-addresses ADR (MOTIR-4206); this asset draws it and invents nothing.

Panel 4 — add a domain · the DNS instruction block, both record shapes

The record set follows the hostname's SHAPE, decided by what was typed (ADR §5): a subdomain gets a CNAME, an apex gets A + AAAA, because a CNAME cannot coexist with the MX and TXT records an apex already carries (RFC 1034 §3.6.2 — marketing-site-hosting.md §3). Both get the _motir-verify TXT, and the certificate is not requested until it resolves.

an apex — A + AAAA

acme-roadmap.com

This is a root domain, so it needs address records rather than a CNAME.

Create these three records

Type Name Value
A @ 66.241.125.x
AAAA @ 2a09:8280:1::x:y
TXT _motir-verify motir-verify-2b7e04…
A root domain is pinned to our IP addresses. If those ever change you will need to edit these records again. A subdomain such as roadmap.acme-roadmap.com uses a CNAME and does not have this problem.

Record shapes and the challenge name are the ADR §5 / §6 decisions, grounded in Fly's own documentation (custom-domain/, custom-domain-api/). The apex caveat is ADR §5's recorded cost, surfaced to the customer at the one moment they can act on it. Values are ILLUSTRATIVE — the real ones come from fly ips list at provisioning (MOTIR-4208 / MOTIR-4214), never a literal.

Panel 5 — the state set · every value of PublicAddressStatus, one row each

All nine values MOTIR-4209's PublicAddressStatus carries are drawn — two subdomain states and seven customer-domain states — and none may be left undrawn. The SIBLING, not a new dialect: this is panel 7's confirm with panel 7's grammar — a plain first line, the consequence in a callout danger on --el-tint-rose, the reversibility last. Three things are added and each is required by MOTIR-4453. (1) The hostnames are LISTED, not summarised. A workspace that renamed twice gives up three names in one click, and somebody who forgot a rename from months ago will not think of the old name as theirs to lose until it is gone — so the rows are the shipped AddressRow, with the same pills the card above draws, and never the words "and any previous addresses". (2) The permanence sentence is not boilerplate and is not drawn as boilerplate — "this cannot be undone" would be true and would say nothing; what is unusual here is that the name stays held against everyone, for ever, by design. (3) The upside is stated, because release is almost always performed BY somebody who wants their projects back on motir.co, and a confirm describing only destruction makes the correct action feel like a mistake. The renames sentence is ADR Amendment 2's releasing does NOT reset the cap in the customer's words — without it the confirm promises permanence and quietly omits the half a customer meets later as a refusal. The destructive action is Button's danger variant, the ONE place --el-danger-text is legal, because it sits on bg-(--el-danger).

What 390 px changes, and what it does not. Nothing is removed and nothing is summarised: every hostname is still its own row, and the permanence callout keeps both its sentences. The three that DO change are named so a builder does not have to guess. The row WRAPS rather than truncating — a long label runs onto a second line inside its own row, its pill staying on the first, and the page never scrolls sideways. The alias pill loses its verb (Redirects here → Redirects), because at this width the deictic costs a line and points at a row directly above it. And the renames sentence drops its second clause; the permanence title carries the load, and releasing does not give them back is an explanation a reader on a phone will not stop for.

WHY THE CONFIRM DOES NOT TRUNCATE, WHEN THE CARD DOES — a decision, not a divergence to tidy up. The shipped AddressRow truncates, and it is right to: on the card the hostname is a LABEL for a row a reader can copy, open or scroll to. In the confirm the hostname IS the message — it is the thing being given up for ever, and a name a person cannot finish reading is a name they cannot decide about. So MOTIR-4455 must NOT reuse AddressRow unchanged inside the modal: it needs the same row with truncate replaced by break-words / overflow-wrap: anywhere. This asset’s own .addr .host has carried that rule since MOTIR-4211, so the departure is consistent with every other confirm on this board rather than new here. Measured, not assumed: rendered at 390 px with a 56-character label carrying no hyphen to break at — the case a hyphenated example silently passes — and read back off the export. The footer wraps rather than compressing the destructive button: a Remove subdomain squeezed to two lines inside its own box is how a confirm’s most important control becomes its least legible.
