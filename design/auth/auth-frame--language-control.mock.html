The language control on the signed-out frame

MOTIR-7746 · a delta of design/auth § The frame every screen inherits. One Combobox in the page's top-right corner, on the wash, outside the card — so every (auth) route inherits it and no card grows by a pixel. Captions under each viewport are measured in Chromium against this file.

1 · Rest /sign-in step 1 · 1280 × 900 · light

The control sits in the page's top-right corner, on the wash, outside the card — positioned against the frame (absolute top-2 right-6), so it is not in the centred column's flow and adds nothing to the card or the page. Trigger: the shipped Combobox trigger at its content width — a languages glyph (aria-hidden), the current language's endonym, the chevrons. Its accessible name is auth.language.label → “Language: English”. The person has already typed their email; the next panels keep it.

2 · Open the list of eleven · keyboard focus on 日本語 · 1280 × 900

The Combobox's own listbox: eleven role="option" rows in lib/i18n/locales.ts order, each named in its own script and carrying its own lang; English is aria-selected="true" with the accent check. The keyboard's active row (aria-activedescendant) is 日本語 — --el-option-active-bg plus the focus ring. The panel keeps the primitive's 256px cap, so the last three rows scroll (panel 13 shows the list at its end). It opens below the trigger, end-aligned, over the wash: at 1280 it never reaches the card.

3 · Switched after choosing 日本語 · the email is still in the field

The same page re-rendered in place: setLocale('ja'), then router.refresh() — no navigation, no reload. The card's client state survives a refresh, so mei.tanaka@example.com is still in the field, and the URL is untouched, so ?next=, ?draft= and user_code are still there. Focus returns to the trigger, which now reads 日本語. Placeholder copy: the Japanese strings are taken from messages/ja.json on this branch and stand in for the catalogue work items' final text; they render in the current Type axis fallback (per-language font sets is a separate card).

4 · Pending between the choice and the re-render · nothing is disabled, nothing moves

The spinner takes the chevron's 16px slot, so the trigger does not change size. The endonym stays the CURRENT language until the page has re-rendered — it is the truth until then. The trigger carries aria-busy="true" and a visually hidden role="status" says “Switching to 日本語…” (auth.language.switching). The form is not disabled: the shipped Settings card sets disabled on the Combobox while pending; this one does not, because a second choice simply supersedes the first.

5 · Failed the choice could not be saved · the page stays in English

A quiet line on the control, not a FormAlert: sign-in still works, and the failure is about the language, not the form. --el-page-bg chip with an --el-border hairline, a circle-alert glyph in --el-danger-on-surface (aria-hidden), the line in --el-text — “Couldn’t change the language.” — and Try again in --el-link, which repeats the same choice. role="status", polite. It clears on the next choice or a successful retry.

6 · Sign-up — both steps /sign-up step 1 (identity) and step 2 (password) · in Deutsch

The control is on BOTH steps because it is the frame's, not the form's. That matters on step 1: Weiter mit Google creates the account from here and never reaches step 2, so the language chosen in the corner is the one the new account is seeded with (the seeding itself is the account-language card). Viewports drawn at 640 wide to fit two side by side; the frame is the same at 1280.

7 · Invite arrival /sign-in?next=/invite/accept?token=… · in 中文

The signed-out half of invite acceptance is this card: proxy.ts sends an invitee with no session to /sign-in?next=/invite/accept?token=…. A switch here is a refresh, so next — and the token inside it — comes back unchanged and the invitee still lands on the accept card. The accept card itself gets no control: it renders under the signed-in shell, follows the ACCOUNT language, and a signed-in person changes language in Settings → Account → Language.

The code stays in the URL across a switch, so the carried banner still names it and the Sign in to continue link still encodes /device?user_code=… into next.

The one screen the frame has no room on: data-auth-wide suppresses the lockup and tightens the padding to fit a 1366 × 648 viewport. 10 · Mobile 375 — rest and open /sign-in step 1 · 375 × 812

The same top-2 right-6 as on desktop: at 375 the trigger's right edge meets the card's, and it ends at 44px — inside the frame's 48px top padding — so it never overlaps the card. Open, the list overlays the card's top as any popover does and stays inside the gutter.

11 · Dark — rest /sign-in step 1 · 1280 × 900 · data-theme="dark"

Every colour is an --el-* token, so the dark palette flips the trigger with the card: --el-page-bg fill, --el-border hairline, --el-text endonym, --el-text-secondary glyph.

12 · Dark — open the list at rest at the top · 1280 × 900
