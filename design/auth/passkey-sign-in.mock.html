Sign in with a passkey — the affordance on the sign-in card's EMAIL step

Story 8.12 (MOTIR-1214) · MOTIR-3609, Surface B. The one control this story adds to the signed-out card, and the panels that say what it does when it is pressed. It sits on the email step, beside the Google button and BEFORE the password step, because verifyPasskeyAuthentication mints a session directly — a passkey sign-in never reaches the password and never reaches TwoFactorChallenge. The card's two-step Clay layout is Story 1.1's and this asset re-specifies none of it; the challenge screen is two-factor-challenge.mock.html and the account-side half is ../settings/passkeys.mock.html. Six panels: where it sits in the flow · desktop · mobile · pending · the two refusals · dark parity.

PANEL 1 · Where the affordance sits — and the two steps it SKIPS. The access-path gate asks the design to draw the door. This door is a BUTTON on step 1, not a route: there is nothing to link to and nothing to deep-link. The two skipped steps are the load-bearing fact for the build — a passkey is not a second factor asked for after a password, it is a replacement for the password, so wiring it into the challenge screen would be wiring it into a step it can never reach.
Step 1 · the door Email Google, the new passkey button, then the email field and Continue. SignInCard · step 'email'
Step 2 · skipped Password A passkey sign-in never reaches it — there is no password to type. SignInCard · step 'password'
Step 3 · skipped Second factor verifyPasskeyAuthentication mints the session itself, so the sign-in never answers twoFactorRedirect and this step never appears. SignInCard · step 'twoFactor'
Step 4 Landed The post-auth destination — ?next=, else /home. resolvePostAuthDestination
PANEL 2 · The email step, desktop — the shipped card with one control added. The button goes DIRECTLY UNDER “Continue with Google”, above the OR rule. That position is the argument: everything above the rule signs you in without typing anything, everything below it is the email path. Putting it below the rule would file it as an alternative to the email field, which it is not. Same primitive as the Google button — Button variant="secondary" size="lg", full width — because they are peers, and a primary here would demote a Continue that most readers still need. Tab order follows the DOM: Google → passkey → email → Continue.

PANEL 3 · The email step, mobile (390px) — a width, not a second design. The card is max-w-[28rem] and the page padding is what changes, so the stack is identical and the new button inherits the full-width rhythm the other two already have. Drawn because 09-signin-mobile.png exists and a new control on that card owes the same check: nothing wraps, nothing is truncated, and the label still fits on one line at the narrowest supported width.

PANEL 4 · Pending — the seconds while the browser has its own sheet open. The button takes the shipped Button loading state: a spinner and a CHANGED label, never a disabled button with the same words. It is the only thing that moves — no page overlay, no skeleton — because the browser is about to put its own sheet over everything, and a second overlay underneath it would be two dialogs for one action. The stand-in below marks where that sheet appears; it is the operating system’s surface and this board deliberately does not specify it.

PANEL 5 · The two refusals — and they take OPPOSITE shapes. A dismissed sheet shows NOTHING: the reader changed their mind, and an error banner would tell them they did something wrong. “No passkey here matches an account” is a real dead end and says so, in the danger callout the card already has for a wrong password — and its copy carries the way OUT, because a reader stuck on this screen needs the next step more than they need the diagnosis.
AUTH_CANCELLED / the reader dismissed the sheet — the card is unchanged.
