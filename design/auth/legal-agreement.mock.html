The sign-up agreement, and the re-consent interstitial

Story 8.4 (MOTIR-657) · Subtask 8.4.15 (MOTIR-3679). Gates MOTIR-1135. Two surfaces and one question: what a person agrees to when they create an account, and what happens when it changes. They take opposite shapes on purpose — a passive statement at sign-up, where agreeing is inseparable from the act; an affirmative one at re-consent, where content/legal/terms.md §14 promises we will not treat silence as agreement. Not an error state: no danger fill and no alert role anywhere.

1 · Where the notice goes — and the gap the render found
Sign-up has TWO controls that create an account, and they sit on different steps. Continue with Google creates one outright from step 1; Create account creates one from step 2. Measured on the running app, the shipped legal line renders on step 2 only — so a person who signs up with Google is never shown the Terms at all, and Art. 13 transparency is owed at collection, which for that path is step 1. The fix is placement, not new copy: the notice moves to the card FOOT, where it renders on both steps.
Step 1 · identity Continue with Google — creates an account → Today: no notice here the Google path sees nothing
Step 2 · password Create account — creates an account → Today: the notice is here below the "Log in" prompt → This card: the card FOOT rendered on both steps
2 · Sign-up · identity step — the notice at the card foot
The shipped step-1 card, unchanged, with one paragraph added below the footer prompt and above nothing. It is separated from the form by the same hairline the other auth cards use for a foot, so it reads as a statement about the card rather than a field in the form. The notice is the LAST thing in the tab order and takes no focus of its own — only its two links are focusable — so a reader who types straight into the email field is unaffected.

3 · Sign-up · password step — MOTIR-1134's line KEPT, re-worded and re-placed
It replaces nothing and adds nothing — there is exactly ONE line, and this is it. MOTIR-1134 shipped legal.signUpNotice here as a pure link ("By creating an account you agree to…"), deliberately not claiming a record was being kept. This card keeps that sentence, moves it to the card foot so both steps carry it, and changes one word: an account becomes a Motir account, which is what makes the same string readable on step 1 where the Google button is the subject. The version accepted is the one served at submit time and is recorded by MOTIR-1135; it is deliberately not printed on this screen, because a semver string next to a sign-up button is noise to every reader and evidence to none.

4 · The rejected alternative — a required tick-box, all three states
Drawn so the decision is visible rather than asserted. The card that filed this one assumed a required checkbox; it is not what ships, for three reasons and one measurement. (a) The lawful basis for the account is Art. 6(1)(b), performance of a contract — consent is not the basis, so a tick-box is evidence, not a legal requirement. (b) Every comparable — Linear, Vercel, Notion, GitHub, Stripe — states it passively at the submit control. (c) It adds a NEW failure mode, drawn third below, to the single highest-value control in the product, in exchange for evidence a submit-time record already provides. The one place it WOULD be right is the interstitial, and there an affirmative button already carries it (panel 5). Keep this panel: an option nobody drew is an option the next reader re-proposes.

5 · Re-consent — one document changed, and the arrival
Signed IN but held. It wears the (auth) frame for the reason two-factor-required.mock.html records: drawing a hold inside the app shell advertises everything the person cannot reach, and a shell that renders is a shell whose data was loaded. Behind it the product is unreachable — with two deliberate exceptions: the legal pages themselves and sign-out. You cannot ask somebody to accept a document you will not let them open. The pill carries its hue in the tint background; there is no danger ink anywhere on this screen.

7 · Declining — the consequence the Terms already promise
This is the half most likely to be skipped and the half a regulator reads first. A decline path that silently does nothing is worse than no decline path, so this screen states the outcome content/legal/terms.md §14 already promises — "If you do not accept it, you may terminate and receive a pro-rata refund of prepaid fees for the unused period" — and then offers the two things a person actually needs. Nothing is destroyed from this screen. Both routes lead to the account surface that owns them; this asset MOUNTS that surface and does not redraw it (the dashed outline is review chrome, not a border to build) — it is MOTIR-1136's, and designed in 8.4.16 (MOTIR-3680) under the account-settings area.

8 · Deferring — "Not now, sign out", and why it is not optional
Every other route is closed to this person, so a screen with no exit is a trap. Somebody who wants to read 4,000 words properly, or ask their own counsel, or who is on a borrowed laptop, must be able to leave without agreeing and without closing anything. It is a ghost button in the foot on every held panel, never competing with the primary action, and the line under it removes the fear that leaving costs something. Deferring is not declining: nothing is recorded, and the same screen appears at the next sign-in.

9 · What does NOT trigger this screen — four of the seven documents
Not a screen — this panel is the DECISION, drawn so it cannot be lost in prose. content/legal/ holds seven documents and comparing all seven would ask every user to re-agree on every routing change. The re-consent set is three: Terms of Service, Privacy Policy, and the Acceptable Use Policy (which content/legal/acceptable-use.md says "forms part of the Terms of Service" and whose own Changes section defers to them). The other four are excluded, each for a reason already published in a document we are bound by — not for a judgement made here.
Cookie Policy We ask for no cookie consent at all — every cookie is strictly necessary or a preference the reader set — so there is nothing here to re-accept. A future non-essential cookie brings a banner, which is what that document itself promises, not this screen.
Subprocessors Terms §14 names "a new sub-processor already covered by the Privacy Policy" as an example of a NON-material change that takes effect when published. DPA customers get the thirty-day objection window in DPA §6 instead — a bilateral notice, not an app-wide hold.
Data Processing Agreement A template, offered on request and signed bilaterally with a business customer. It is not part of what an individual accepts at sign-up, and it is amended with the customer who signed it, through its own §6 and §11.
Model providers A factual roster that changes whenever a gateway channel is enabled. It varies no commitment and carries no notice period — the decision record says so in as many words (docs/decisions/legal-document-set.md §7, amended 2026-08-27).
10 · Mobile (390px) — a width, not a second design
The card is w-full max-w-[28rem] inside a px-6 page, so below the sm breakpoint the column is the viewport minus 48px of gutter. Two things were MEASURED here rather than asserted, and the second came out the other way. The notice's two links survive the wrap: the sentence breaks with a link ending each line and neither collides. The changed-document row's version chip does not hold the title line at this width — it wraps to its own line beneath the title, left-aligned with it, because the title is a wrapping flex. That is the intended degradation, and it is DRAWN rather than described so nobody builds it expecting one line and reaches for a truncation to force it.
