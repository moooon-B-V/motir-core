Asking Motir to revise the plan you are REVIEWING

A reviewer holding a plan they want shaped differently has two levers today, and both are terminal: approve it as it stands, or decline the whole tree and start over. The commonest reaction to a generated plan is neither — it is nearly right — and there is nothing to press. This asset draws the third verb.

It draws ONE composer at the foot of the rail, TWO rows on the timeline that already renders, one HELD state on a button that already has one, and one chip in a row cluster that already has three. It does not redraw the plan review canvas, the proposal peek, the approve / decline controls, the staleness summary, the decided outcome or the plans list, it adds no second planning workspace, and it introduces no colour that is not already in the palette.

2

AT REST — the ask, NEXT TO the two verbs and visibly not a third one

THE ACCESS PATH. The reviewer is already on /plans/<id>; this story adds no route and no door to reach. The door it does add is this one, and it is drawn in place rather than described: a one-line composer sitting inside the bottom-anchored decision block, above Approve and Decline, separated from them by the rail's own --el-border rule.

Why it is NOT a third button. Approve and Decline are terminal — each ends the review — and a third control in that stack would be read as a third way to end it. A text field is not a verb of that kind: it asks for an instruction rather than a decision, and a reader who has just read two buttons does not mistake an input for a third. So the relationship the card asks to be VISIBLE is carried by the rule and by the form, not by a label explaining it.

It composes the shipped PlanChangeComposer — with the `@` trigger SUPPRESSED, which is a real change and not a styling choice. That trigger opens useWorkItemTargetSearch, which searches the project's COMMITTED work items. A revision is anchored at the plan, and the things it can name are PROPOSALS, which have no key to mention. Offering the picker here would search the wrong universe and return rows the instruction cannot act on. The composer therefore gains one prop (mentions={false}) and the plan-revision case does not fork a second input.

3

IN FLIGHT — the plan is still `planned`, and Approve is HELD rather than offered

This panel draws what MOTIR-3596 decided, and decides nothing itself. AMENDMENT 10 D2 closes the approve / revise race with a LEASE on the plan: while a revision holds it, approvePlan and declinePlan are REFUSED, the tree is untouched, and neither act cancels the other — the loser retries. So the reviewer is never told their revision was thrown away, and never told their approve half-landed.

The rail says that with the grammar it already has. Approve and Decline take the shipped disabled treatment, and the hint beneath them is REPLACED — exactly as it is replaced today for a generating plan — with the reason. This is the card's own criterion that a reviewer is never offered a verb that will be refused, applied to a second condition rather than to a second mechanism.

And the optimistic hold is not the whole answer, deliberately. A lease can be taken between the render and the click, so the disabled state is a courtesy and not a guarantee. The refusal that arrives anyway lands in the rail's SHIPPED role="alert" line above the gate — one sentence, in place, with the plan still readable. Two mechanisms, because the client cannot know the answer and the server can.

LANDED — the timeline says WHAT happened and WHO; the count says how much

Two rows, in the sequence and the grammar Part X fixed — one label, one actor clause, one timestamp, no badge and no second treatment. The pair BRACKETS the revision, which is also what makes them the lease (MOTIR-3596): Revision started with no Revision landed after it, inside the window, is a held plan. So the reviewer learns a revision is running by reading the timeline they were already reading, and the product needed no second place to record it.

The MODEL is not in the clause, for the reason Part X measured: at the rail's 298px text column a model name costs every row a second line, and the header carries it once already. It rides the row's title. 5

WHAT CHANGED — a revised proposal read against an unchanged one, in the LIST

The list, not the canvas — and that is a decision, not a convenience. The canvas already has an emphasis mode: Part IX's Show changes rings every PROPOSAL on the level and dims the committed cards around it. It answers which of these is proposed? A reviewer coming back from a revision is asking a different question — which of these moved since I looked? — and putting a second ring on the same canvas to mean a second thing would give one surface two highlight languages. The list groups by op and carries a per-row chip cluster already, so the recency fact goes there.

The marker is the SHIPPED Pill, in the SHIPPED slot. The row's right cluster already renders a Stale pill, a status pill and the op chip; a Revised pill joins it in front of the op chip, at severity="info" — the hue in the tint background under --el-text-strong, the recipe every other chip on this row uses. The op chip does not change: op says WHICH kind of change this proposal is, the pill says THAT this one moved. Orthogonal, like Part IX §L2 — neither is an alternative to the other.

a11y. The composer is a real <form> with an accessible name; the field's name TRACKS its placeholder, which is the shipped composer's own contract. The in-flight band is not an alert — nothing failed — so it is ordinary content inside the rail's existing live region, and its state is carried by THREE cues that are not colour: a word, a glyph, and two controls that changed. The Revised pill contains its own word, so no row's state is conveyed by colour alone. The held buttons keep their labels and gain no title-only explanation — the hint beneath them is real text.

Page state after the revision lands (the enforced contract). The revision arrives from a JOB while the reviewer sits on the page, and /plans/[id] is a Server Component, so router.refresh() re-runs planReviewService.getPlanReview and IS what updates the canvas, the list, the rail's item count, the timeline and the Approve state. What it cannot reach is any client island seeded by a useState(initialProps) initializer — the List/Canvas switcher and any expanded proposal own theirs — so those need a tick they watch, not a refresh. The UI card enumerates each surface and names which mechanism carries it in its pull-request body; assuming the refresh covers all of them is the recurring bug that contract exists to prevent.
