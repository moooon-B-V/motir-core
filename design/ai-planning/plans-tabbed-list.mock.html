Part III §3's rule is REVERSED here, and the reasoning that failed is worth keeping. It read: “A DECIDED row shows the DECIDER, not the requester”, on the ground that two bare person names in one line are ambiguous — the two-chips-read-as-one hazard applied to people. That hazard is real. What was wrong was the assumption that the two names would land in the SAME entry.

They land in different ones, and the entry is what says the role. The decider rides the WHEN entry, where panel A has drawn it since 843 — approved yesterday by Mara — with the verb in front of it. The requester rides the ATTRIBUTION entry, behind its avatar, exactly as on an undecided row. So the line reads 8 items · approved yesterday by Mara · ⟨av⟩ Jonas · via Motir AI, and no reader has to work out which name is which: one is preceded by approved … by and the other by a face. Part III's three-entry cap is untouched — the requester goes INSIDE entry 3, it does not add a fourth.

And Part III's own ⚠️ is what makes this a fix rather than a preference: the decider was drawn in panel A and NEVER SHIPPED — PlanRow renders t(view.whenKey, { when }), so today a decided row names NOBODY. The rule was protecting a collision that could not happen because half of it did not exist. Building the decider is this Part's other half, and row 8 is why it cannot be assumed present: a plan the abandoned-plan sweep terminated (MOTIR-3236) is declined with decidedById NULL, and its row correctly reads declined 2 days ago with no by at all — the same absence, never a placeholder rule Part III set for the attribution itself. Neither party is ever carried by colour or glyph alone: every one of them is a name.

RE-MEASURED FOR FIVE TABS (MOTIR-3577), and the disposition below CHANGES. Rendered headlessly from the REAL shipped Segmented with the real labels, through the real app/globals.css + @motir/design-system theme. The strip's width is intrinsic and identical at every viewport. The four-tab numbers this panel recorded reproduce exactly — 310.3px with labels (segments 84.3 / 67.7 / 75.7 / 70.6) and 358.8px with counts — which is what makes the new pair trustworthy: 361.9px with labels and 422.6px with counts, the Stale segment costing 49.6px bare and 61.8px with its count.

So the labels no longer fit. The authed shell is px-4 below sm, so 375px leaves a 343px content box: 310.3 < 343 < 361.9. The rule this panel set — all tabs keep their labels at every width — held on the assumption that the strip fits, and the fifth member falsifies it. Its other half stands untouched: counts still render from sm up (422.6 < 592), and never below.

The disposition is the SCROLLER, and this REVERSES the rejection above. That rejection was argued, not measured — a scroller would push Declined off-screen on the surface whose job is to show which statuses exist. The measurement retires the premise: the overflow is 18.9px, so Declined is not off-screen, it is clipped by a fifth of its width, and a half-drawn word is a stronger "there is more here" cue than any chevron this surface would have to invent. The strip scrolls the SELECTED segment into view on mount, so a deep link to ?status=declined opens on its own tab.

The three alternatives, drawn and rejected. A flex-wrap on the track is 343px wide and 80px tall — it strands Declined alone on a second row and breaks the one-of-N reading a segmented track exists to carry, while eating twice the vertical space above the list on the smallest screen. Shortening Generating to Writing fits (337.8px) and is a lie: the tab and the row's own pill would then say different words for one status. And glyphs are still out, for the reason this panel already gave — five lifecycle states have no icons a reader could tell apart.

⚠️ A finding this re-measurement surfaced, which is NOT about the fifth tab. The 343px box is a 375px viewport. At 320px the box is 288px, and the FOUR-tab strip is already 310.3px — so /plans overflows its gutter on the smallest phone today, silently, and has since MOTIR-3241. The scroller fixes that case too, which is why it is the right instrument rather than the cheapest one.

The header measurement, for the record. At 375px the shipped header WRAPS — flex-wrap drops the Plan-with-AI pill onto its own line under a subtitle that has already wrapped to two. Removing the pill (panel 1) removes that wrap, which is a second reason for the removal beyond the duplication.

A plan goes stale when work it proposes to change has since been finished. None have.

Rose, an octagon, and NO accent border. The pill is the shipped Pill severity="danger" exactly as the component paints it — --el-tint-rose under --el-text-strong, hue in the tint, charcoal ink (finding #35). It is deliberately NOT the four legacy status pills' saturated hue-ink, which predates that finding; an implementer follows the component, not this asset's older rows. The icon square takes --el-tint-rose, the one tint no status spends (sky / lavender / mint are taken and declined uses --el-muted). OctagonAlert was chosen over CircleSlash, Ban and ShieldAlert: the two slash glyphs read forbidden rather than stuck and are indistinguishable from each other at 16px, and ShieldAlert reads security. An octagon is also still separable from the advisory AlertTriangle at that size, which is the pairing the second row above has to survive.

The accent border stays OFF. planned earns it because it means awaiting your approval. A stale plan cannot be approved, so the same border would invite a click that fails. The rose square and the danger pill carry this one needs you without promising the button works.

The DECLINED row is reproduced here on purpose. It is the treatment this status must not be confused with — a quiet --el-muted square and a nearly fill-less tone="archived" chip, which says ENDED. Stale is not an ending: it is a plan still in the queue whose exits are wait or decline.

Both at once is a real row, not an edge case. After AMENDMENT 9 the advisory count and the status are one engine at two severities, so the peach 2 may be out of date chip and the rose Stale pill share a row and say two different things: some proposals drifted and this cannot be approved. ⚠️ The shipped staleCountFor short-circuits on status !== 'planned', so as written it would render this row with NO advisory count — the row most likely to have one. Part XI §7 lists that with the three other silent fallthroughs.

The zero tab is drawn for the reason panel 3 already gives — the strip's job is to show which statuses exist, and a tab that appears only when populated teaches a reader that the vocabulary changes under them. Its empty state names the state and then says in one sentence what would put a plan there, the same register Nothing generating uses; it carries no CTA, because there is no action that makes a plan stale.

The whole complaint behind MOTIR-3560 is answered HERE, not by the pill. A reviewer who can see precisely which proposal went stale and has no way to say so is not helped by a chip in a list — so the rail PLACES the answer: one Pill severity="danger" per fatal proposal, directly under the outcome line, each carrying the proposal's own title and the target that closed under it. Capped at three with a +2 more tail, because the rail is a column and the first three are enough to tell a reviewer whether this is one stale edge or a plan that has been overtaken.

The same chip goes on the item node in the canvas, on the offending proposal's own card, so the canvas and the rail agree without the reader cross-referencing two lists.

The outcome line is not declined's. That one reads Plan declined — your tree was left untouched, an ENDING. This one has to say what happened AND what is left to do, and it must not offer a repair that does not exist: AMENDMENT 9 establishes there is no stale → generating edge, because a proposal cannot be withdrawn or re-targeted. So the copy names the two real exits — decline it, or wait in case the work reopens — and Approve is drawn DISABLED rather than hidden, so the reader can see that the button they came for is the one that is unavailable.

The rail is rendering a list it is already handed. Under AMENDMENT 9's UNIFY disposition the staleness engine reports these as a FATAL target_terminal reason beside its existing advisory ones — this is a second RENDERING of one verdict, never a second computation.

⚠️ The WHEN is missing on purpose, and it is a schema question. The rail cannot say when the plan went stale: plannedAt is the close and decidedAt is a decision a stale plan has not had. Part XI §3 hands MOTIR-3578 the choice — add a Plan.staleAt column and render it, or state that the rail omits the moment. Nothing here assumes the column exists.
