The PROPOSED to-do list in the peek’s PROPOSAL MODE

When a plan proposes a manual work item, the proposal already carries that card’s ORDERED STEPS (docs/decisions/agent-authored-plans.md AMENDMENT 13). This asset draws where the reviewer READS them, one click before approving: a read-only To-do list in the peek’s main column, composed from the row grammar design/work-items/todo-list.mock.html settled and the peek chrome peek-proposal-mode.mock.html settled — neither is redrawn. Full reasoning: design-notes.md Part XV.

Panel 1 · §1 · the placement, measured

1 The section in the peek’s main column — five steps, 1440×900

The peek is IssueQuickViewPanel in PROPOSAL MODE (Part XIV), opened on an un-materialized add that carries todos. The list is a section in the MAIN column, after the explanation and before nothing: the peek defers children and comments to a page a proposal does not have (Part XIV §2), so the steps are the last thing in the column and the reader reaches them by scrolling the body they were already reading. It is the shipped ContentSectionCard header grammar, so it reads as the same section the created card will carry — which is the whole point: what is approved and what arrives must not differ.

Panel 2 · §2 · the row, in its READ face

2 What is composed, and what is DROPPED

The row is todo-list.mock.html’s grammar with the write track removed. The three-track grid becomes TWO — checkbox, body — because the actions column has nothing to hold. The checkbox stays and is inert rather than being removed: it is what makes the section recognisable as the list the reader will later tick, and a proposal has no doneAt to draw. Copy stays, because copying a command is a READ.

the SHIPPED write face (todo-list.mock.html)

  • Add the key to Fly secrets You

Four write affordances: the reorder grip, edit, delete, and the add row — plus a checkbox that ticks.

the READ face this asset specifies

  • Add the key to Fly secrets You

All four are gone. The checkbox is drawn, unchecked, aria-disabled, and does not respond — a control that looks tickable and is not is worse than no control, so it is dimmed to --el-input-readonly-bg rather than left at full contrast.

Panel 3 · §3 · the states

3 Absent · one step · instructions expanded

An add with no todos, or with [], renders no section at all. That is Part XIV §1’s rule applied to a new row: a row’s absence is a statement about the SUBJECT, and an empty To-do list · 0 of 0 would assert that a planner considered the steps and proposed none — a claim the data cannot support.

Panel 4 · §4 · at scale

4 Twelve steps — the main column scrolls, the rail does not

Twelve rows is past the granularity bar’s comfortable range and well inside what a real provisioning card carries. The section is NOT capped and does NOT get its own scroller: it grows, and the MAIN COLUMN scrolls, which is the one scroll surface the peek already has (QuickViewMain is overflow-y-auto). A second scroller inside it would give the reader two things to move and no way to tell which one they are in.

Panel 6 · §6 · drawn as NOT drawn

6 A modify and a remove show no list

This panel exists so nobody adds one later by accident. It is not an oversight and it is not a gap to fill: a modify’s target is a COMMITTED card, its list is a person’s PROGRESS, QuickViewData carries no to-do field (lib/dto/quickView.ts at d2a0c964b), and AMENDMENT 13 D2 refuses a todos on a patch. The steps are shown on an add, which has no live card to fetch them from, and on nothing else.

Panel 7 · §7 · dark

7 The same section in dark

No token is redefined for dark: the section is the same markup under data-theme="dark", so the executor pill, the mono command box and the inert checkbox all take the dark values the palette already defines. The inert checkbox is the one to check — --el-input-readonly-bg against --el-card must stay distinguishable from the interactive one on the created card.
