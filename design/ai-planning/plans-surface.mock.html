Who asked for this plan, and who wrote it

AMENDS panels A and B — one meta entry carrying TWO parties; the row, its pills, the staleness flag and the approve / decline bar are UNCHANGED
A plan has THREE parties, and they are commonly three different people. A teammate asks for it, an agent or Motir writes it, a lead approves it. Only the third was ever recorded (decidedById, drawn in panel A as “approved yesterday by Mara”). The first is the one a reviewer wants first — somebody's credential produced this; whose? — and an agent-authored plan makes that question sharper, not softer: the MCP token belongs to a person, and a Motir generation was clicked by one. So the attribution names the PERSON first and the agent second.

While a plan is undecided, “who asked” is what you weigh; once it is decided, “who decided” is the operative fact and the requester is history, so the row drops it and the detail header keeps it. That rule is also what stops two bare names landing in one line, where a reader cannot tell which one holds which role.

What truncates, in order. The plan TITLE keeps its own single-line ellipsis and is never shortened by the attribution — the meta line sits below it and wraps. Inside the attribution, the PERSON truncates at 10rem and the HARNESS at 12rem, each with its full value on title; the avatar, the “via”, the item count and the timestamp always stay legible. The meta line is flex-wrap, so a narrow row moves the whole attribution to its own line rather than pushing anything out.

The status tag is an OVERLINE, above the title (MOTIR-3074). Plan titles are GENERATED — long by default, and routinely carrying an unbreakable token (a SCREAMING_CASE constant, a repo name, a cuid). Beside the title, a shrink-0 tag took a third of a 22rem rail: the title wrapped to five lines, the vertically-centred tag landed inside its text column, and the title's own longest word pushed the rail past its track (measured: 7px of overflow at the shipped width). On its own line the title owns the full width and a five-line title reads exactly like a one-line one — and the state stays the first thing read, which is what the tag is for. The title additionally carries min-w-0 + overflow-wrap: anywhere, which is owed whatever the placement.

The header SPELLS the roles; the row does not. The row is scanned, and an avatar in front of a name already reads as “this person's” — so it says Mara · via Claude Code. The header is read once, by the person about to press Approve, and there the words Requested by / written by are what stop the two names being taken for one party. The header also carries the model, which the row omits: it is the difference between two agent plans, and nobody scans a list on it.

This is the shipped roadmap level — the breadcrumb walks the committed ancestors, the parent's children fill the canvas whether or not anything depends on them, and same-level blocked_by edges draw as they always do. The proposal is one more card at that level wearing the add style Panel B already specifies.

So "root or parented?" stops being a question the reader has to ask. A proposal under a committed parent is drawn INSIDE that parent's level, among its siblings, with the parent named in the breadcrumb. A genuine root is drawn at the top level, where there is no breadcrumb to walk. The two cases look different because they ARE in different places — no badge, no chip, no extra signal to learn.

Edge case — a parent that is archived or hard-deleted: the level cannot be opened, so the proposal falls back to the top level and the breadcrumb has nothing to walk. That is the honest rendering, and it is the same one a genuine root gets.

The step keeps its own height and its own content — MOTIR-1782 keeps every decision it made about what the step SAYS. The canvas takes the remainder with min-h-0 so it shrinks rather than pushing the band out, and it is never replaced. Once the step settles it collapses to its shipped one-line form and the canvas has the pane, with no extra rule.
Why this re-opens a decision instead of editing a file. MOTIR-1775 / MOTIR-1782 decided the swap deliberately, and the shipped prop doc says why: “once the plan has materialized, the canvas of proposals has served its purpose, and replacing it is the truthful use of the space.” That is correct on its own premise — a proposal IS spent by the decision that resolves it. This report overturns the premise, not the conclusion: after MOTIR-3160 and MOTIR-3161 the pane holds the RECORD of the decision, not the proposals, and a record is produced by the decision rather than spent by it.
And they are different KINDS of thing. The establish step is a TASK — MOTIR-1782's own central claim is that its default path is one sentence, one primary, one quiet secondary. The canvas is a RECORD. A task and a record can share a pane vertically; only two records compete for it. That is why replacing was reasonable when the pane held a spent artifact and is not once it holds a record. MOTIR-3073 already trimmed this swap for a project that ARRIVES with code; Panel H finishes the same sentence for a project that does not.

A DECLINED plan proposes no repository set, so there is no band at all and the canvas has the pane. Panel H changes nothing for a decline — Panel G is the whole of that state's fix.
