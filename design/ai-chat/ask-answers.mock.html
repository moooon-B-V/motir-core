Ask about this project — the cited answer turn, and the chrome that follows it

MOTIR-1815. A turn in the one conversation can now be a question instead of a plan change. Nothing about the surface changes: the same workspace, the same rail, the same composer, the same single door. What changes is that a turn has an intent, that intent is resolved by the server and comes back on the turn, and the canvas chrome follows the latest turn rather than a mode nobody ever chose.

This asset composes; it does not redraw. The two-pane frame is planning-workspace.mock.html (MOTIR-1193); the rail shell, its bubbles, its markers, its composer and the confirm bar are plan-change-conversation.mock.html (MOTIR-1727) and plan-change-planner-speaks.mock.html (MOTIR-2225). None of those are re-specified here. This asset owns four things and nothing else: the answer turn's form (panel 2), the canvas footer across the two turn kinds (panel 3), the correction marker and the redirect hand-off (panels 5–6), and the callout row's copy (panel 1).

Two things above this asset fix what it may draw, and both are cited rather than re-opened. design-notes.md § “EVERY ROW OPENS THE SAME SURFACE” — every row shares ONE href, a row is a LABEL and not a route — and docs/decisions/conversation-turn-intent.md (MOTIR-1816) §1/§5 — intent is server-resolved, the client sends none, and it belongs to the turn, never to the session. So there is no ask/act switch below, no ?mode=ask, and no chrome a thread is locked into. The assistant avatar's glyph is the sibling glyph design's element (MOTIR-3183), drawn here at the 13 px it specifies inside the 28 px circle and re-decided nowhere.

⭐ The row seeds NOTHING into the composer. The STARTER SET grows by one. The shipped spec permits a row to “seed the composer's starter phrasing”, and the rail already has the mechanism for exactly that: STARTERS — chips that prefill the composer on click and never send. The opener widens. Shipped copy is “What should change?”, which is now half the truth and quietly discourages the other half. It becomes “What should change — or what would you like to know?”. One sentence, both capabilities, still one thread.

An answer is an ordinary assistant bubble — same fill, ink, avatar and width as the opener and the planner's findings report. It needs no new treatment to read as Motir speaking, which is the finding MOTIR-2225 already made and this asset inherits rather than re-litigates.

The decision: inline, not a trailing source list

Inline wins on three grounds, and the first is decisive.

1 · The rail already cites inline. An assistant findings report renders its [KEY](motir:<id>) tokens through MarkdownView → WorkItemRefChip — the shipped component comments say so in the imperative: “never a second inline treatment invented for this surface.” A trailing source list would be a second citation treatment on the same bubble in the same rail. There is no reading of the shipped code where that is the smaller change.

2 · The mirror links inline. Rovo and Linear both render references in the sentence. The numbered-footnote model belongs to a full-page answer surface; in a 332 px rail a bibliography routinely out-lengths the answer it follows.

3 · Checkability. A citation next to the claim tells you which sentence rests on what. A trailing list tells you only what was read — which is the weaker guarantee, on a surface whose entire promise is that you can check it rather than trust it.

The count line is not a second list. citations — the string[] the envelope carries — is the grounding CONTRACT, and the answer may rest on items the prose never names. One quiet line inside the bubble, “Answered from 6 work items”, states the size of the evidence base without re-rendering it. It is a number, and the two treatments cannot be confused.

⭐ The chrome follows the LATEST TURN — one footer slot, two contents

the drawing this asset exists for

The finding — measured in the shipped component, not assumed

PlanningWorkspaceHost mounts the confirm bar as a shrink-0 flex SIBLING below a min-h-0 flex-1 canvas box, conditionally: {state.review && !state.decided && !index.isEmpty ? <PlanChangeConfirmBar/> : null}. So today the bar mounts and unmounts, and the canvas box grows and shrinks by its full height every time.

That would be nearly harmless if the canvas were a static page. It is not. ProjectRoadmapCanvas anchors three control clusters to the BOTTOM of that box — the engine's zoom + fit cluster at bottom-4 left-4, the LOCATE control at bottom-4 left-[8.25rem], and the full-screen control at right-3 bottom-4. Unmounting the bar slides all three down by the bar's height; mounting it slides them back up. Alternating between a question and a change — the exact rhythm this story invites — would make the canvas's own furniture hop on every turn.

The nodes themselves do not move: there is no ResizeObserver and no fit-on-resize in the canvas, so pan and zoom survive. The jump is entirely in the bottom-anchored chrome — which is why the fix belongs to the box, and costs nothing else.

The marker sits under the ASSISTANT turn — the moment the user discovers the mis-read — as a single centred line in the vocabulary plan-change-planner-speaks.mock.html already established for turn markers. Two labels, one per direction, each naming the act and never a mode:

· under an answer → “Propose changes instead”
· under a proposal → “Answer this instead”

What it does Why
Re-runs the ORIGINAL user turn under the other intent. No second user turn is appended. The person said one thing, once. The transcript is a record of who said what — the posture MOTIR-2226 set.
Appends a NEW assistant turn. The superseded one stays, undimmed and unstruck. A correction is a second answer, not an erasure. The reader has to be able to see later that Motir read it wrong.
Sets the user turn's intent to what ran and intentCorrected to true. The thread records what Motir DID — the isAnswer precedent, not a re-derivation.
Is not a mode. The next turn is classified from scratch. §5. Nothing in this thread is now “in” anything.

One marker at a time. Only the LATEST assistant turn carries it — a superseded answer's marker is removed when its correction lands, so a thread never offers two ways to re-run the same user turn.

Presentation. Centred, 11 px, --el-link, underlined at text-underline-offset: 2px — an interactive marker, distinguished from the passive ones (--el-text-secondary, no underline) by ink AND underline, never by colour alone. It is a real <button>: in the tab order, --focus-ring-color on focus-visible, and while the re-run streams it becomes the disabled marker “Re-reading…” rather than disappearing.

And a line replaces it once it fires: “Re-read as a plan change” / “Re-read as a question”, in the passive marker style, above the new turn — so the transcript says why a second assistant turn exists.

6

The redirect — one turn, two streams, and no gap between them

ADR Consequence 3

Every turn goes through POST /api/ai/ask. When the handler's first pass decides the turn was a plan change, it returns no answer and intent: 'plan_change'; core then dispatches the shipped plan-change submit. So a plan-change turn streams twice. Without a drawn state for the hand-off, the rail would show a spinner, stop, and start a different spinner — which reads as a failure and a retry.

The rail draws ONE continuous waiting state across both jobs, and names the hand-off in the marker line rather than in the bubble:

·
turn 1split the blocked story into three

Sent to Motir AI

stream 1 — reading

Reading your request…

Reading it as a plan change — working on the proposal

stream 2 — planning · the SAME bubble, text swapped

Working through the plan…
Rule Why
The waiting bubble never unmounts between the two jobs. Its text is swapped in place, exactly as the footer slot's is. A bubble that disappears and returns reads as “that failed, it's trying again.” Nothing failed.
The hand-off is a marker, not a bubble. “Reading it as a plan change — working on the proposal”. It is provenance, not conversation — the same reason the shipped submission marker is a marker.
The footer slot stays RESTING until the proposal exists. It changes when the review lands, not when the redirect is announced. The slot's contract is “is there something to decide right now” — and during stream 2 there is not, yet.
A failure in EITHER stream lands in the shipped error state. Motir does not fall through to the other intent. ADR §4 — spending a plan generation on a turn whose reading failed is the expensive half of the asymmetry.
An ask turn shows only stream 1. There is no announced hand-off, so a question's waiting state is exactly what shipped. The common path pays nothing for the redirect's existence.

What the user is told, and what they are not. They are told the reading changed, because it explains the wait and it is the fact the correction marker in panel 5 acts on. They are not told about two jobs, two models or two prices — that is the boundary's business (ADR Consequence 6 bills it honestly; nothing in the rail itemises it).
