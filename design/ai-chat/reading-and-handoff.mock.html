Reading your project, and the two doors out of the plan window

MOTIR-4766, under story MOTIR-4753. The plan window now OPENS for a project that has never been planned (MOTIR-4765) instead of ejecting it to /onboarding, and the first thing that happens inside it is that Motir READS what the project already has. Three outcomes follow, and the planner decides which — it is not a count, a threshold or a marker (MOTIR-4767). Either the project can be planned and the session simply plans it, or the user is moved to one of two onboardings and brought back here afterwards (MOTIR-4770). One row, drawn twice, because the read tells the surface which to draw. readOnboardingSubstrate stops at ONBOARDING_SUBSTRATE_ITEM_CAP = 200 and reports the overflow, so at the cap itemCount is a FLOOR and not a total. Rendering it as an exact 200 work items would be the surface making a claim the read explicitly refused to make — and it is the same number the planner's judgement is about to be based on.

It names the things, and that is the whole design. A spinner says something is happening; this says I am reading acme/widgets, acme/widgets-api and 214 work items, which is the product demonstrating in one sentence the thing this story argues for. The rows are the four fields readOnboardingSubstrate returns (MOTIR-4756) and nothing else — repositoryConnected puts a repository row on screen, repositoryIndexed decides whether its sub-line can say Code graph ready, and itemCount is the third row.

NOT A PROGRESS BAR, and the constraint is MOTIR-4768's own: nothing here knows a duration. A bar has a track, a fill and therefore a claim about how far along it is, and it would have to keep that claim once a model call is in the middle of it. Three quiet dots and usually takes a few seconds promise only what is true. And itemCountTruncated is why the count is worded carefully — at the 200-item cap the row reads 200+ work items, because the read itself says the number is a floor.

The return is a FOURTH moment, not a mirror of the hand-off. The hand-off says we need something first; this says we have it — what do you want to plan? So it is not another full-card interstitial: the workspace is simply THERE, ready to do what the user pressed the button for, and the acknowledgement is one line in the conversation. The line sits in the RAIL because the rail is where the session speaks — a toast would fade, and a banner over the canvas would put chrome between the user and the thing they came back for.

⚠️ AND THE PLANNER ASKS — it does NOT hand over a plan (Yue, 2026-09-06). An earlier revision of this panel drew the canvas full of dashed proposals and a footer reading 42 proposed work items, with the planner opening on "here's a plan across acme/widgets and all 214 of your items". That is not what a planning session is. A regular session arrives with the context and WAITS to be told what to plan — which is exactly what continue means (MOTIR-4767: the session asks and plans exactly as a regular session does). So the canvas shows the project AS IT STANDS — the items that are already there, no proposals — the footer says what the session can SEE rather than what it has produced, and the composer is live and empty. The same correction removed design/onboarding-migrate/'s Panel 0, which drew the identical mistake at the end of the wizard.

The earlier turns are still above it, and that is the design claim MOTIR-4770 AC5 makes checkable: the conversation is a persisted server thread (usePlanChangeConversation's resume payload, re-read on mount), so the user comes back to the session they left rather than a new one that happens to look similar. Nothing in this asset warns about losing a draft, because at the moment of the move there is nothing to lose — the verdict is decided before any pass runs, so no proposal and no typed message can exist yet.

Panel 6 · WHAT IT MUST NOT LOOK LIKE — and the outcome that draws nothing at all (continue: no hand-off, no interstitial, just a session)
Not an error

"We couldn't plan your project."

Nothing failed. The read worked, and it produced a finding. A failure frame turns the most informative moment in the journey into an apology for a bug that does not exist.

Not a refusal

"This project isn't ready for AI planning."

A judgement about the project reads as a judgement about the person who made it. The finding is about what Motir can SEE, and the sentence has to be about that: there's nothing here yet for me to plan from.

Not a dead end

A card whose only control is Close.

The user pressed Plan with AI. Every surface in this asset carries a way onward and says where it leads; the return (panel 5) is what makes that a promise kept rather than one merely stated.

And the third outcome — continue: the surface that isn't drawn

A full repository with rich context means no onboarding at all, and no hand-off surface either. The reading state (panel 1) resolves straight into an ordinary planning session — the regular framing, the regular questions, the planner waiting to be told what to plan — and the user never learns that a decision was made about them. That is the success case, and it draws nothing, which is worth stating explicitly here because an asset full of interstitials invites a builder to add a fourth one saying Good news, we can plan this! Nobody needs to be told that the thing they asked for is happening. continue makes the run a regular planning session from its first system message (MOTIR-4767), and the surface's only job is to get out of the way.
