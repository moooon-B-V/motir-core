At the cap — a ten-line paste stops and scrolls inside itself

The cap is 8 rows (184px), measured. Past it the field stops growing and overflow-y flips from hidden to auto, so the content scrolls within the field and the footer's height is bounded. The cap is a VERTICAL budget and the split changed WIDTH, so the number does not move — but its worst case is re-measured here rather than inherited. Worst-case footer = cap + target tray + running bar: 184 + 66 + 56 = 306px at 480px, 327px at the 352px floor (the tray's third line) and 274px at the 720px ceiling (the tray on one line). At a 900px-tall viewport the 480px worst case still leaves the transcript ~525px — the majority of the rail — which is the argument the 8 rows were chosen on, now true at the width the composer actually ships at.

The three decisions, the anatomy, and what is unchanged

Everything the code card reads literally.

0 · THE WIDTH IS 480px, AND THIS CARD DID NOT CHOOSE IT. It is defaultRailWidth(1440) = clamp(352px, 33.333%, 50%) of the split container, from MOTIR-6249's approved design result as shipped in lib/planning/railWidth.ts (RAIL_MIN_PX 352, RAIL_DEFAULT_FRACTION 1/3, RAIL_MAX_FRACTION 0.5). Panels 1–11 are that 480px; panel 12 is the 352px floor, and the floor is not an edge case — below the 1056px crossover a third is under the floor and the default clamps to it, so every split container from 768px to 1056px opens at exactly 352px. The plan page's revise box is NOT a split host and keeps its own width; measured, its 378px default behaves like the 480px one and not like the floor.

1 · The cap is 8 rows (184px) — unchanged by the split, and re-measured at it. Confirms the story's assumption. The cap is a VERTICAL budget and the split changed WIDTH, so the number does not move; what moves is the worst case around it, re-measured on sheet 3 as 306px at 480px (cap 184 + tray 66 + running bar 56), 327px at the floor and 274px at the 720px ceiling. At 480px on a 900px-tall viewport that still leaves the transcript ~525px — the majority of the rail — which is the argument the 8 rows were chosen on, now checked at the width the composer actually ships at. A wider field does not mean a taller one: it means the same paragraph reaches the cap LATER — measured, the sample message is 3 rows at 480px and 4 at 352px.

2 · @ and Send align to the BOTTOM from two rows on (items-end on the control row, bottom-1.5 on the trigger). Centre-aligning walks both controls down the field as it grows, so the target a person is aiming at moves while they type; bottom-aligning holds them at a fixed distance from the caret's last line. It is also where MUI X's chat composer puts its send control — in a toolbar below the field. The price, stated rather than buried: at REST the row is 44px and Send is 32px, so bottom-aligning drops Send 6px lower than today's centring. That 6px is the whole visible change to the untouched composer, and it buys a control that never moves again from one row to eight.

3 · NO persistent keyboard hint. Checked against two references. MUI X's Chat Composer documents the behaviour — "submits on Enter and inserts a newline on Shift+Enter" — and its component anatomy carries no hint element; the behaviour is implicit. Slack documents "Create a new line: Shift Enter" in its help centre, not as composer chrome. At the 352px FLOOR a permanent hint line costs ~18px of the one region that never scrolls, permanently, to teach a convention both references treat as already known. (Both checks are of published documentation, not of a rendered screenshot — stated so the next reader knows what was and was not verified.) No message key is owed, in either locale.

4 · The manual resize handle is GONE with auto-grow (resize-none, replacing the primitive's resize-y). The two fight: a hand-dragged height is overwritten by the next keystroke's measurement.

Primitives composed. The shared Textarea (MOTIR-6237 gives it autoGrow + maxRows), Button (primary/secondary, sm), Spinner, PlanningTargetChip, BrandMark. Nothing is hand-rolled.

Tokens. Shape through --radius-input|card|control|badge|btn, --spacing-input-x|y, --spacing-chip-x|y, --height-input, --height-btn-sm. Colour through the --el-* element layer only. The two numbers this design adds — 20px and the 8-row cap — are the text-sm line-height and a row count, read at runtime from the computed style rather than typed as pixels (MOTIR-6237's contract), so the clamp stays exact under every type scale and density.

Unchanged, and drawn so: the tray, the answer bar, the running bar and Stop, the read-only replacement (planning-read-only, which sits in the composer's slot and is not a field at all), the revise note, the placeholder set, focus-on-open, and the draft kept across a view switch.

GIVES / TAKES. This design GIVES MOTIR-6238 the cap, the bottom alignment, the whitespace-pre-wrap + wrap-anywhere bubble, the no-hint decision and the two widths to build and test at (480px and the 352px floor); it GIVES MOTIR-6237 the row arithmetic and the resize-none switch. It GIVES MOTIR-6210 panel 8's pre-filled shape. It GIVES MOTIR-6239 and MOTIR-6240 the floor as a second width their seams and their E2E walk are exercised at, which panel 12 is the drawing of. MOTIR-6249 and MOTIR-6250 are CONSUMED, not amended: this asset reads their settled numbers out of lib/planning/railWidth.ts and chooses none of them. It TAKES nothing from any card: no criterion on 6237, 6238, 6239, 6240, 6206, 6210, 6249 or 6250 is contradicted or removed by it — the cap it settles is the number those cards already say they read from this asset, and it is unchanged. The one correction this redraw makes is to ITS OWN first version's prose, not to a card: the target tray's "three or more chips at 22rem" threshold, falsified by the render (sheet 4).
