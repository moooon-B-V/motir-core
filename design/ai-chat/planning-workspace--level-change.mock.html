A pending change to the LEVEL you are standing in

A delta on design/ai-chat/planning-workspace--arrival.mock.html (MOTIR-6159, the design of record for this surface) and its notes section The canvas ARRIVES INSIDE the node being planned. Only the panels that change are drawn. The overlay frame, the scrim, the canvas·rail split, the exit chrome, the arrival rule, the target crumb, the follow-move and the node-level diff frames are all inherited unchanged.

The problem. Since MOTIR-6154 the canvas opens INSIDE the target, so the target is the LEVEL and not one of base.nodes. decoratePlanChangeLevel frames nodes ON a level, and a level has no node to frame — so a proposal that renames, re-parents or removes the target draws nothing at all, while the confirm bar counts it. The person is asked to confirm a change they cannot see.

THE DECISION — why the band, and not the other two

MOTIR-6223 names three candidates and deliberately does not choose between them. This is the choice, with its reasons. Chosen: candidate 2, the level header — drawn as a second ROW INSIDE the breadcrumb bar rather than as a band of its own, so it adds no new object to the canvas.

Candidate Verdict Reasoning
1 — on the breadcrumb CRUMB rejected Three reasons, and the first alone settles it. (a) The crumb is already spoken for: MOTIR-6159 put the planning-target mark there — the target glyph plus the 2px accent underline — and a second mark on the same element would have the crumb saying two different things (this is what the planner is pointed at, this is about to change) in one 18rem box. (b) A crumb cannot carry the diff LINE. What the reader needs is not something changed but title and status changed, to this, and the crumb has nowhere to put it — it truncates at max-w-[18rem], so the mark would be the first thing to go. (c) The crumb is NAVIGATION. Clicking it navigates; a diff mark on a control whose press does something unrelated invites a click that does nothing about the change. And remove is undrawable there outright: a struck-through link to a level that is about to cease is incoherent.
2 — a level header / banner CHOSEN It is the only candidate that can carry the whole node vocabulary — the state chip, the word, and the diff line naming what changed — which is exactly the information the missing frame would have carried. It represents the LEVEL as an object, which is the thing that has no drawing. Placed as a second ROW inside the breadcrumb bar, because that bar is already the one element on screen that stands for the level (that is MOTIR-6159's own answer to MOTIR-2070), because it inherits a card that already exists rather than adding one, and because at narrow width it is the only canvas chrome that survives at all (panel 7).
3 — extend the follow affordance rejected Its sentence is false here. “Plan is in ARP-1 · Go there” means the plan is somewhere you are NOT; when the plan changes the level you are standing in, the plan is exactly where you ARE, and a control offering to take you there would be offering to take you where you already are. Extending it to also announce the level's own change would give one control two opposite meanings. It is still the right answer to the other half of MOTIR-6223 — a proposal that lands BESIDE the anchor — and panel 5 composes it there unchanged, because there its sentence is already true. Composing it is not extending it.

What this does NOT draw, and why. diffStateForItem has a fourth state, locked — a terminal card the proposal cannot touch. The band does not draw it. The band's subject is a pending change, and locked is the absence of one; a level with no pending change draws no band at all (panel 4, state D), and saying “nothing is changing here” in words would be a line every reader has to read in order to learn nothing. The rail already says it once, in the planner's own voice: “Anything already finished is left untouched — I can't change done work.”

Axis Specification
What changes Only WHEN it is armed. Today it appears after the canvas DECLINES a follow request (the reader navigated first). It also appears when the pending proposal places nothing on the level the reader is standing on and places something on another level — which is the same sentence, reached a second way. Its copy, its tokens, its placement and what pressing it does are all untouched.
Which level it names The level carrying the proposal. Where several do, the one holding the most proposals; ties go to the nearest common ancestor of the reader's level, which is always reachable by crumb.
Band and affordance together Possible, and correct: this level changes AND the plan also lands elsewhere. The band is row two, the affordance stays in row one pushed right by its own margin-left:auto. They never contend for a slot.
At the root There is no bar at the root — measured: the canvas renders a breadcrumb only when drilled. MOTIR-6159 already decided the affordance brings the bar with it (the bar renders with the root crumb alone); the band does not, because at the root the level is the project, and a project is not a work item a plan can change.
6

SEVERAL changes at once — the band must not compete with the frames

The level changed, a child was added and a child is going. Three statements, and the reader has to be able to tell which is about WHERE THEY ARE and which is about WHAT THEY SEE. They never compete, because they are on different surfaces at different scales: the frames are on 280×124 cards on the recessed board; the band is one 12px row of chrome on the raised bar. The band also carries no COUNT — counts belong to the confirm bar, which owns the whole plan rather than this level.

NARROW width — where the bar is the only chrome left

Measured, at a 720px viewport: the workspace stacks, the canvas pane collapses to a ~110px strip that shows no nodes at all, and the breadcrumb bar is the only canvas chrome a reader can still read. That is the strongest argument for putting the level's statement inside the bar rather than beside it or under it: a band docked to the canvas's bottom edge, or a header below the bar, is invisible at this width. The bar is flex-1 min-w-0 under its basis-[44rem], so it narrows to the gutters — measured 704px wide at 1440, 696px at 720 — and the band narrows with it.
