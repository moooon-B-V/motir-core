Settings at ARRIVAL — the family frame

The 31 app/(authed)/settings/** routes share one body shape, and this is the pending drawing for it — the FAMILY shape of design/shell/design-notes.md § WHICH SURFACES EARN A FRAME rule 3. It is a shared COMPONENT each route renders IN-PAGE after its own gate, never a loading.tsx above them: five of the eleven authed routes that call notFound() are inside this family. Drawn over the REAL settings rail, the two REAL shipped settings skeletons, and the real en copy.

Panel A — the frame in situ, and the rail that is not in it

The rail is identical in both frames, and that is the finding. SidebarNav variant="rail" is mounted in app/(authed)/layout.tsx's AppLayout sidebar slot — it is not inside {children} at any settings route, and neither of the family's two layouts (settings/project/layout.tsx, settings/account/layout.tsx) renders one. So the rail cannot be pending, and the frame is the PANE ONLY. The header is identical too: it is a translated string plus the project name, both already resolved by the gate.

Panel B — the pane frame alone, and what each part is

  block what it is why it is on that side of the boundary
1 the header the real one — <h1> + <p>, no placeholder Rule 2: a frame only covers a region with NOTHING to show yet. A settings title is t('<pane>.title') and its subtitle interpolates a name the gate resolved, so both exist before the first pane read is issued. This is where the family frame differs from the generic one, which draws a title block because a generic page's title is not knowable.
2 the card stand-in one animate-pulse region: a bordered card with a label bar, an action bar, and three icon+text rows the shape every settings pane's first card takes. Copied from nothing — it IS settings/project/fields/loading.tsx's composition with its two header placeholders removed.

Three rows is not a count of anything. It is a screenful for a 42–46rem pane and it is what both shipped settings skeletons already draw.

Panel C — the WIDTH axis: the frame takes the route's own max-w

A settings pane is a CENTRED column, so on this family a width mismatch IS a visible shift — horizontally. The shell asset's no-shift claim (heights and gaps, never widths) holds where the frame and the page both fill <main>. Every settings pane instead opens mx-auto max-w-[Wrem], and the family uses eight distinct W: 34, 42, 45, 46, 48, 52, 60 and 64rem. A frame fixed at 42rem in front of a 60rem pane settles by 144px on each side. So W is a prop, and the route passes its own.

Panel D — the two shipped settings skeletons, and the verdict on them

SUPERSEDED — replaced by the shared component, not merely joined by it. The two files are the same composition twice, and they have already drifted: the title bar is w-32 in one and w-40 in the other, the action bar w-24 and w-28. Neither number stands for anything — the real titles are Fields and Components, which the family frame now paints instead of approximating. Two hand-rolled copies of one drawing is exactly the drift rule 4 of the earn-a-frame rule names.

what happens to it and why
Both files are deleted by the code card, in the same commit that lands the shared component in those two routes. Not because a route boundary is illegal there — neither page calls notFound(), so both are legal. Because after the shared component they draw the same thing worse, from a second source, and a boundary that survives beside an in-page frame shows the reader the same pending state twice for one navigation.
Nothing else under app/ changes. app/(planning)/loading.tsx and app/(public)/explore/(square)/loading.tsx are outside this family and outside this story.
