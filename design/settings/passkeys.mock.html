Account › Security — passkeys (sign in with your fingerprint, face or device PIN)

Story 8.12 (MOTIR-1214) · MOTIR-3609, Surface A. The passkeys section of /settings/account/security: register a passkey, see the ones you hold, rename one, remove one. Built on Better-Auth's @better-auth/passkey plugin, registered by MOTIR-3610 — every control here is an endpoint that exists. It sits in its OWN card, deliberately NOT as a third row inside “What you'll be asked for”: a passkey replaces the password rather than following it, and a screen that files it under “second factor” teaches the reader the wrong thing about what they just turned on. The pane's own chrome is reproduced, not re-specified — design/settings/two-factor.mock.html owns it. The sign-in half is a separate asset, ../auth/passkey-sign-in.mock.html. Seven panels: zero · populated · registering · rename · remove · refusals · dark parity.

PANEL 1 · /settings/account/security — no passkeys yet. The pane a reader reaches the first time. THREE cards now: the pane’s own shipped lead (“two-factor authentication is off”), then the NEW passkeys card, then the shipped methods list. Above, because a passkey is the thing we would rather they chose, and because it is the only method on this pane that makes signing in both safer and faster. The empty state spends its words on what a passkey IS: most people have never knowingly used one, and “Add a passkey” alone answers nothing. The rail is unchanged — this card is a new SECTION on a pane that already has a door, so no nav entry is proposed.

No passkeys yet

A passkey is a key your device keeps and unlocks with your fingerprint, your face or your PIN. Nothing is typed, so there is nothing to phish, reuse or leak — and because unlocking it proves it is you, one touch is already two factors.

Your laptop, phone or a security key can hold one. Motir never sees it.

PANEL 2 · Three registered — the steady state, and the layout the build should treat as canonical. Each row carries the four things that let a reader act: the NAME they gave it, whether it is Synced or This device only (the plugin’s deviceType, in words rather than in its own vocabulary), when it was added, and the two controls. Two rows have to be tellable apart and the name is what does it — which is why the register flow proposes one rather than leaving it blank (panel 3). Note the methods card below now carries a fourth row: Passkey, read-only, pointing back up. That row is TwoFactorStatusDTO.methods containing passkey (MOTIR-3611) and it is present even though two-factor is OFF — a passkey counts on its own.

PANEL 3 · Registering — the seconds between “Add a passkey” and the browser’s own sheet. A pending ROW inside the card, not a modal: the browser is about to put its own sheet over everything, and stacking a dialog underneath it would be two overlays for one action. The copy does one job — tell the reader their own device is about to ask them, and that Motir never sees the answer. The name field is NOT here: the plugin registers first and the default name is applied with it, so renaming is a separate, unhurried act (panel 4). The default is read from the browser at registration (“MacBook Pro”, “Chrome on Windows”) so the list is legible before anyone types anything.

The browser's own passkey sheet appears here, over the page.
It is drawn by the operating system, not by Motir: we cannot style it, position it or read anything from it. It is stood in for so the sequence is legible, and deliberately not specified.
PANEL 4 · Rename — a MODAL, decided here rather than left to the code card. Inline edit was the alternative and it loses. The row is already dense (name, chip, date, two controls) and an inline field either pushes the controls out of reach or grows the row mid-list; a modal also gives the 64-character bound somewhere to be STATED rather than silently enforced. The value is the current name, selected, so the common case is type-and-save. PASSKEY_NAME_MAX_LENGTH is the number in the helper.

PANEL 5 · Remove — the confirmation, and the consequence in the copy. Removing a passkey is instant and cannot be undone from Motir’s side, so it confirms. The warn callout is CONDITIONAL — it is drawn only when this is the reader’s LAST passkey, because that is the case where the consequence is not obvious: they go back to a password. With two or more left the modal is the same shape without the callout. The destructive action is the primary button in danger ink, and “Keep it” is the escape.

PANEL 6 · The three refusals a reader can actually see — and the one that shows nothing. The plugin’s PASSKEY_ERROR_CODES has fourteen members; these are the three that reach a reader on THIS surface, and they take three different shapes. The third is the one a build gets wrong: a dismissed browser sheet is a decision, not a failure, and it returns quietly to the resting card shown at the bottom of this panel.
