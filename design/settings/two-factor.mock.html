Account › Two-factor authentication — authenticator app · email fallback · recovery codes

Story 8.11 (MOTIR-1213) · MOTIR-1216. The self-service 2FA surface: enrol an authenticator app, keep email as a labelled lower-security fallback, hold ten single-use recovery codes, and revoke the devices you told Motir to stop asking. Built on Better-Auth's twoFactor plugin, which MOTIR-1217 already registered — every control here is an endpoint that exists. Six panels: off · on · enrol · recovery codes · low-codes, turn-off and errors · dark parity. The login challenge is a separate asset, design/auth/two-factor-challenge.mock.html.

PANEL 1 · /settings/account/security — nothing enrolled. The pane a reader reaches the first time. The rail's Security group now holds TWO rows and this is the first of them — that row is the access path, and it is the only door to this surface. The empty state does one job: say what a second factor buys and make the authenticator the obvious first step. Email is not offered yet, because until a second factor exists there is nothing to fall back FROM.

PANEL 2 · Enrolled. The steady state, and the layout the build should treat as the pane's real shape. Four cards in one column: what is on, the recovery codes, the devices that stopped being asked, and the way out. Three things are deliberate — the PRIMARY method is stated in words rather than implied by order; the email row keeps its "lower security" Pill even when it is switched ON, because the caveat does not stop being true; and the recovery counter is a number a reader can read at a glance, because "2 of 10 left" is the sentence this card exists to say.

PANEL 3 · Set up authenticator app. Two steps in ONE modal, with the rail at the top so the reader knows there is a second step before they scan. Step 1 shows the QR AND the manual key, because the reader may be running the authenticator on the machine displaying the code — a QR alone strands a desktop 1Password user. Step 2 is the confirming code: this is what makes enrolment two-phase, and it is what stops a mis-scanned QR from locking somebody out of their own account. Nothing is enabled until step 2 succeeds.

PANEL 4 · Your recovery codes. The one moment the plaintext exists — the stored form is encrypted, so this modal is the only place these ten strings will ever be readable. The copy says “we can’t show them again” and NOT “we can’t read them”, because the second would be false: the plugin stores them encrypted, not hashed, and a design that overstates a security property teaches the implementer to overstate it too. Dismissal is gated on an explicit acknowledgement, which is the only affordance that survives a reader who closes modals by reflex. Beside it: the regenerate confirmation, which is destructive and says so.

PANEL 5 · Low, exhausted, turning off, and wrong. Four states the resting pane cannot show. The recovery counter is the interesting one: at 2 it warns and at 0 it is a real, dangerous state — 2FA on with no way back in if the app is lost — so the card says so rather than rendering a quiet zero. Turning 2FA off is a step-up (a password re-check), which is the standing rule for every management action on this pane; and a wrong confirming code keeps the reader on the step they are on with the field marked, never bounces them back to the QR.

PANEL 6 · Dark parity. The same enrolled pane under data-theme="dark", unchanged markup — every colour resolves through --el-*, so the theme flips and nothing is redrawn. Worth a panel because two of this surface's chips carry MEANING in their tint (the mint "On", the peach "Lower security", the lavender "Primary"), and a tint set that collapses to one grey in dark would take the distinction with it.
