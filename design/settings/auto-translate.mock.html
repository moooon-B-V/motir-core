Translation — Auto-translate work content

The project-settings room where a project admin turns auto-translate on or off, where every other member reads what it is set to, and where anyone can see who changed it and when. And the rail row that opens it. Card MOTIR-8822.

The control that returns when it is off is the predecessor’s per-work-item Translate button (the design published with MOTIR-8112); the per-field and per-title reading surfaces are drawn by their own cards.

Panel 0 · The access path — a new row in the Automation group, between Hosted agent and Rules

The room is reached the way every project-settings room is: its rail row, and the matching settings-translation command-palette deep link the registry lights at the same time. It sits in Automation, directly under Hosted agent — the rooms that configure what Motir does on this project’s behalf, and what it costs, sit together — and above Rules, which is user-authored automation and stays last. The glyph is Languages, the one the per-work-item Translate control carries.

Panel 4 · No AI credit — the setting is on, or is being turned on, in an organization with no translation credit

The wording is the consent; the money itself is another page’s, linked and never rendered here.

Panel 6 · The door to the refused state — drawn for the record; no actor reaches it

A room whose view key an actor does not hold renders the area’s shipped NoAccessState inside the chrome, with its own section sentence (settings.noAccess.section.translation). With project:browse as the view key it is unreachable: an actor who cannot browse never reaches the area (the project’s not-found), and every built-in role that holds the write key holds the view key (the closure check, in the notes). A custom role built without browse is the one way here.
