NEW — the Job runs fold-in section. The fifth capability, and the one that never had a home here. ⚠ THERE IS NO WORKSPACE RAIL ON THIS SCREEN, AND THAT IS THE POINT. The first version of this asset drew a one-row workspace rail here, because /settings/workspace/jobs answered at every workspace count while its siblings 404’d. That exemption was an unfinished collapse, not a decision: the route had never been given the fold-in its three siblings have. With the section above it, all four surfaces hide together and docs/decisions/organization-tier.md §6d — a hidden tier may not remove a capability — is satisfied by RELOCATION, which is what it asks for, rather than by an exception.

Nothing is stranded on the other side of that threshold. Below two workspaces there is no workspace switcher, so there is no door — and no room: all three /settings/workspace/* routes notFound(), and Name, Members, Leave workspace, the require-2FA policy and the job-runs dashboard are all reachable on /settings/organization for a plain org member who is a workspace member. So the rail’s bottom section keeps NEITHER workspace row — the sibling card MOTIR-4845 removes both, on the assets that own that section, and a workspace-tier row inside the PROJECT rail is the tenancy mismatch MOTIR-4640 removed for the Git row a week earlier.

ONE filter axis, revealedOnly, it DEFAULTS CLOSED, and it applies UNIFORMLY to all three rows. The organisation registry has two (what the actor HOLDS, what the BUILD has); this tier has neither — every route here is membership-gated with no role above it, and none is cloud-only. What it has instead is the threshold, so the axis is the reveal. It defaults closed for the reason its two siblings default closed: a surface that forgets to thread it drops the row rather than offering a door onto a route that notFound()s.

⚠ THE THIRD ROW USED TO BE AN EXCEPTION, AND THE EXCEPTION IS RETIRED — said here because a reader who knows the old behaviour needs to see that it was removed rather than overlooked. /settings/workspace/jobs answered 200 at every workspace count while its two siblings notFound()ed, and that was written down as a property of the route (MOTIR-3502 AC 6). It was an unfinished collapse: the route had never been given the fold-in the other three workspace surfaces have. The FOLD-IN card builds it, this row becomes reveal-gated like its siblings, and the axis stops carrying a carve-out — which is what lets panel 4 draw a below-reveal state with no workspace rail in it at all.

The index row is called Workspace, not Settings — the word the switcher’s own row uses for the same route. Inside a settings area a row named “Settings” names its own container; the organisation rail has the same row and calls it Organisation, the project rail calls it Details. The SWITCHER keeps its word: it is outside the area, where “Workspace settings” is what the destination IS.

The security and jobs glyphs are CARRIED, not chosen — read out of app/(authed)/_components/SidebarNav.tsx’s bottom section, where those two rows render <ShieldCheck /> and <ListChecks /> today. Two doors onto one room that disagree about its icon are two rooms.

The workspace glyph is this asset’s own choice, and it is Boxes: a workspace is the CONTAINER its projects and teammates live in, so its glyph is a set of things held together — where Building2 (taken by the organisation in organizationSettingsNav.ts) is the company itself. Stated because the family resemblance is real and deliberate rather than accidental: the project rail’s components row carries the singular Box, one tier down, in a rail that never renders beside this one.

The third group is operations, and the name is a decision. general and access mirror the organisation’s. Job runs is neither: it configures nothing and gates nobody — it is where you look when something did not happen. It gets its own group rather than being filed under general because a group whose rows all filter away is not rendered, which is exactly what panel 4 needs to be true in reverse: at one workspace it is the ONLY group that survives, and a third group is what makes that arm cost nothing.

The rail HEAD follows the ORGANISATION’s shape, not the project’s. Three heads exist — SettingsSidebarHeader (the project, a ProjectSwitcher), AccountSidebarHeader (the user, a static identity) and OrganizationSidebarHeader (the organisation, a static identity). This one is static: a switcher in a settings rail would let you change WHICH workspace you are configuring from inside the pane, and switching a workspace re-points the active project and navigates away (afterContextSwitchTarget) — a context switch buried in a configuration surface. The switcher stays in the top bar, where it already is and where panel 5 draws it. The TILE is a square with --radius-control, matching the organisation’s: the circle is this product’s PERSON grammar, and a workspace is not a person. Its fill is --el-tint-sky where the organisation’s is --el-tint-lavender, so the two tiers are told apart by hue at a glance when a reader moves between the two rails.
