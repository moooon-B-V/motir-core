PANEL 2 · The export, all four states. Requested → preparing → ready → failed, drawn as the card body changes. The ready state is the one the card asked for: what the reader RECEIVES is a file in this pane, not a spinner and not a link in an email. The reason is measured, not stylistic — a private object is served through the authenticated content route, which 302s to a presigned URL minted per request with a 300 s TTL (signedDownloadUrl, lib/blob/uploader.ts). A URL that dies in five minutes cannot be emailed to somebody, so the email carries the news and this pane carries the file.

PANEL 3 · The confirmation — the decision this card exists to make. The dialog is a LEDGER before it is a confirm box: three groups, each with the reason it belongs there. Deleted is what you solely own. Anonymised is the case the Privacy Policy has already decided — a contribution to someone else’s conversation is not yours alone to erase, so your name goes and the thread stays. Kept is the part a confirmation must never promise away: invoices outlive an erasure because Dutch tax law requires them to. Drawn at the shipped ceiling — max-h-[90vh] (691 px on the 1366×768 floor), size="lg", Modal.Body scrolling with the head and footer pinned by the flex column — so the ledger is CUT OFF at the fold on the left, which is what the product actually renders. That cut is a feature: the type-to-confirm field sits at the BOTTOM of the scroll, so the button cannot be reached without travelling past the facts.

PANEL 4 · Blocked — and the block is drawn on the pane, never raised at submit. The card asked for “the sole-admin workspace” case; the shipped code says the blocking tier is the organization, not the workspace, and the measurement changes the design. assertNotLastOwner refuses to remove or demote the last org owner, so a sole owner of an org with other members genuinely cannot be deleted — that is the hard block, and the way out is make someone else an owner. A workspace behaves differently: LastMemberError only stops the last MEMBER from LEAVING, and any member may delete the workspace outright, so sole-membership is a choice shown in the ledger (panel 3), not a blocker. Two different mechanisms, two different treatments — and the button is disabled with the reason beside it, so the reader never types an email address into a form that was always going to refuse.

PANEL 5 · Scheduled — the grace period, and where it is cancelled. The product already holds the doctrine this state is built on: docs/decisions/code-graph-index-fleet.md §14.3 says “a grace period the user cannot reach is not a grace period”, and gives a workspace delete none for exactly that reason. An account delete is the opposite case — the reader can sign back in — so the window is real and the design has to make it reachable. Hence two doors: this pane, and an app-wide banner on every page, because a reader who changes their mind will not think to come back to Settings › Data & privacy to do it. Signing in is the way back to this state, not the cancel — it used to be both, which made this panel a drawing of a failed cancel rather than of the window (MOTIR-3742).

PANEL 6 · Dark parity. The same pane at rest under data-theme="dark", with data-appearance-scope beside it so the Tier-3 --el-* layer is re-emitted on the subtree and color declared ON the panel rather than inherited — both are required or the headings render near-black on near-black. --el-danger as heading ink is the one thing to check here: it measures 4.25:1 on the dark page, which clears the 3:1 graphics bar but not the 4.5:1 text bar — so the danger signal on this card is carried by the 2 px border and the trash glyph, and the card heading is a normal --el-text in dark. Light keeps the red heading, where it measures 4.51:1.
