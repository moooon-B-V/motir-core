The universal approval frame — a port you review in, and the verbs beneath it

MOTIR-4789, second cut. The universal element is the frame, not the button: what a person learns once is what REVIEWING looks like. Three bands — a header saying what you are looking at, the PORT that renders it, and the verbs below. The port's contents vary by kind; the frame does not. Its states, routing and record are settled by docs/decisions/approval-gates.md (merged, 9840d00ea) — where that record and this asset disagree, the record wins.

Withdrawn from the first cut: the rule that the control sits at the HEAD of the section. It moved the button further from the thing it decides. The frame removes the problem that rule was trying to solve.

0 · Placement — the frame IS the section

The frame does not sit inside the Design result section; for a card with a gate it is that section. There is no second place to look and nothing to scroll past to find out whether a decision is owed.

1Header — what you are looking at, which version, when it arrived, and the state. Same shape for every kind; only the words change.
2The PORT — the subject, RENDERED. A floor height so it is never a sliver, a ceiling with its own scroll so it never pushes the verbs off screen, and Expand for a subject that deserves the whole viewport. This is the band the whole story is named after.
3The verbs — below the port, because you decide after you look. Beside them, in words, what approving will DO.

The port's CONTENT is the only thing a new gate kind supplies. That is what makes the frame universal, and it is why the merge kind needs no second control, no second confirm and no second decided treatment.
