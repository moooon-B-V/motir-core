situation 3 · two workflows — nothing quoted

Why this is a choice

Your requirement allows two workflows

Your requirement says “customers can get their reports” and also “finance downloads them at month end”. One reading is a report emailed as it is made; the other is a page of past reports kept for download. Both fit the words, and they are different products.

It sits directly under the question, ABOVE the options, because it changes how the options are read: overruling what you said (1), improving on what you said (2), and resolving a fork in your own requirement (3) are three different questions. The situation is a --el-tint-lavender chip — deliberately not the best if you want chips' sky, so the two never read as one vocabulary. Situations 1 and 2 quote the decision on a You said line; 3 quotes nothing and says where the requirement forks. Why select-then-commit satisfies "the verbs are the options themselves". Each option IS a verb: choose(optionId). What N buttons would do is put the options in TWO places — once in the port with what they are best for and their WHY, once in band 3 as bare names — and a person would press a name, which is the guess the best-for line exists to prevent. Selecting in the port presses the option where its reason is written; band 3 then carries ONE commit verb that names it. The shape is identical at 2 and at 6. Before a pick the verb is drawn DISABLED with its reason as the consequence line (Panel 1a) — the frame's shipped honest-disabled rule (MOTIR-5678), not a new one.
