3 · ci_credits_exhausted — ADMIN. The two-option DECISION (amendment §D; Yue’s directive). Two peers with equal weight and no default — neither is .btn-primary, because one keeps the hosted arrangement and one ends it, and dressing either as “the answer” is Motir’s thumb on the scale. Each states its real cost: Add-credits states §B’s resume latency (“within a minute, at most 15”) instead of implying instant; Move-the-repositories states the GitHub account, the asynchronous transfer and the App re-install — never “one click”. The takeover is never gated on a stored GitHub identity: no admin has one (motir-core’s only social provider is Google), so gating it would hide it from everyone. No disabled “Add credits” button is ever rendered — a control a user cannot use is worse than a sentence explaining why. Shown beside the admin view at the same width so the split is visible.

8 · Where the decision SITS on the page — measured, and the one ordering rule this amendment adds. In the normal order the Motir CI card is third, and it starts 756 px down the page: on a 1280×800 laptop (≈700 px usable) a paused admin would land above the decision and have to scroll to find it — which fails §D’s constraint in practice even though the block itself fits. Rule: when state === 'ci_credits_exhausted', the Motir CI card renders FIRST in the stack; otherwise it keeps its third position (panel 1). This orders by urgency without adding a second decision surface, without a page-level banner to keep in sync, and without changing the CONTENT of ① or ② — they are byte-identical, just below. Measured below: the card top sits at 85 px, both consequence lines end by 367 px and both option buttons by 415 px — the whole decision sits in the top 60% of the fold, with the untouched ① line visible beneath it.
