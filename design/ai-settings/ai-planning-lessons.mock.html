The takeaway is the row. A lesson has four text fields; only title is on the row, because the question a reader is answering here is "do I want to open this", not "what does it say". The row is one line at 52rem and wraps to two at the narrowest supported width — it is never truncated with an ellipsis, because a half-sentence rule is worse than a long one.

Two numbers, and they answer different questions. lastOccurredAt answers is this still live; recurrenceCount answers how settled is it. A lesson seen four times is a pattern; one seen once is an observation. They are rendered as prose ("Last seen 2 days ago", "seen 4 times") rather than as a date and an integer, because both are read for their meaning, not their value.

The axes are chips, and an empty axis is not drawn. Motir routes a lesson by card kind, work type and plan phase, and an axis a lesson leaves empty means unconstrained — so a lesson with no axes at all shows one Applies to every card chip rather than three empty ones. Each chip carries its axis name, because "story" alone reads as a status.

The retire affordance appears on hover and on focus, and is a labelled button, not an icon: it changes what the planner is told, for everyone on the project, from that moment on. Its label is Stop applying — what the reader wants to happen — never "Retire", which is the column's name and not the reader's word. Built by MOTIR-3330; drawn here because the row's shape has to make space for it.

Panel 2 — one row, and why it shows what it shows. Every element on the row earns its place by answering a question the reader actually has before opening the lesson. The retire affordance is drawn here, in its hover face, because it is what decides the row's right-hand gutter — a list drawn without it is a list that gets redrawn when MOTIR-3330 lands. It is a labelled Button and not an icon: an unlabelled ban glyph beside a rule reads as "this rule is broken", which is the opposite of what it does.

Panel 3 — a lesson that is not being applied, and the TWO reasons. They are different acts and the surface says which: Not applied is somebody's decision, recorded on the row; Not seen in 90 days is the passage of time against lastOccurredAt, and it reverses itself the moment the mistake recurs. Collapsing them into one greyed row would make a lesson somebody switched off indistinguishable from one that simply has not come up lately. Neither is removed from the list — the whole point of the surface is to show what the planner has stopped saying, which is why the API returns retired rows the injection path filters out. A not-applied row keeps its full text, loses its emphasis (--el-text-tertiary, and the chips drop their fill), and its action inverts to Apply again. It is never struck through: the lesson is not wrong, it is just not in force.

Name a sibling by its work-item key, never by description

kindstorytypecodephaseskeleton
Motir is applying this. It goes into every plan drafted for this project.
What happened

A card named the work it depended on in prose — "after the sibling bug lands" — instead of naming that work item. Nothing could read the sentence, so the card entered the ready set with its prerequisite still open, and the run that picked it up built against a base that could not satisfy it.

Why it matters

A dependency written as a phrase is invisible to everything that decides what is startable. It reads to a person exactly as a real edge does, which is what makes it expensive: every signal says the card is ready, and the only thing that disagrees is the build.

How to apply it

When a card's body or its acceptance criteria mention other work, name it by its key and wire the dependency. If the work has no key yet, that is the thing to fix first.

Panel 4 — the detail. The screen that answers why is the planner telling me this?, so it carries the reasoning rather than a summary of it: all four text fields, under labels in the reader's words — What happened · Why it matters · How to apply it · Where it came from — never the column names body / why / howToApply / sourceRef. Reduced to a title in a table a lesson becomes a rule handed down without justification, which is the same opacity in a nicer font. Its own route (lessons/[lessonId], the second declared drill-down) rather than a modal, because a lesson is the kind of thing one person sends another. The status callout at the top is the SAME .callout box the AI-planning page already uses for its guardrail line — no new primitive. Recorded from shows the work item the correction came out of, which is the provenance a reader can actually follow.

Motir hasn't learned anything here yet

When a plan turns out to be wrong — a missing dependency, a card sized past what one run can finish — Motir writes down the correction and applies it to every plan it drafts for this project afterwards. Those corrections appear here, with what happened and why, so you can read them and decide which ones to keep.

Nothing is recorded until a plan is actually corrected. Recording can be switched off in AI planning settings.

Panel 5 — the empty state, which most projects will see for weeks. It is the moment the feature explains itself, so it is written as an explanation and not as a placeholder: what would appear here, when, and where it comes from. It does NOT say "No lessons yet" and stop — that sentence tells a reader who has never seen this mechanism nothing at all. The last line names the switch that turns recording off, because a reader who has just learned that Motir watches their planning should not have to search for the control. Same .lrows container as the populated list, so the surface does not change shape when the first lesson arrives.

Panel 6 — dark. Both surfaces on the dark palette, with data-appearance-scope beside data-theme="dark" so the Tier-3 --el-* layer is re-emitted on the subtree, and an explicit color on the scoped container so headings that only INHERIT their ink resolve against the dark layer rather than against :root.
