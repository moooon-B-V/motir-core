Job runs — the DELIVERY state

The operator surface at /settings/workspace/jobs ships today and has never had a design asset. This is the first one, and it exists because a sibling card needs to add one column to it: the job ledger says whether the provider ACCEPTED a message, and nothing anywhere says what became of it. Panel 1 is the surface as it stands; every panel after it is the change.

Three of them may have bounced, been spam-foldered or been refused outright — this surface cannot tell you, and cannot tell them apart from the one that arrived. That is the defect, and it is invisible precisely because everything looks fine.

Panel 2 · The change — one column, after Status

Delivery is its own column, immediately right of Status, and never a second pill inside it. They answer different questions — Status is did our job run, Delivery is did the message arrive — and a run can succeed while its message bounces, which is the entire point. Two pills in one cell would read as one fact with two moods. Non-email.send rows show —, the same em-dash this table already uses in Failure and Duration, so the column costs a reader nothing on the rows it does not apply to.

Panel 3 · Every value, and what it means

The enum is the checklist: five values plus the no-row case, each drawn, each with the tone it takes. All five are the Pill primitive — no new component — and every coloured one carries its hue in the TINT with --el-text-strong ink, which is the recipe that clears AA in both themes. Only accepted is neutral, and deliberately: it is the absence of news, not good news, and colouring it green would restate the lie this column exists to end.

Value Tone What it tells the operator
accepted tone=neutral The provider took the message and has said nothing since. The starting state of every row — and, for a message sent before this shipped, the permanent one.
delayed severity=info Deferred and still being retried by the provider. Not a failure; not yet an arrival.
delivered severity=success The receiving server accepted it. Not "read", and not "in the inbox" — a spam-foldered message is delivered.
bounced severity=danger Refused. Nobody received it, and re-sending to that address will not help.
complained severity=warning A person marked it as spam. Peach, not rose: it arrived and was read — the damage is to sender reputation, not to this message.
— (no chip) No delivery row: a job that is not email.send, a send that predates the record, or a send the provider refused outright (its Status is already failed).

Panel 4 · The row detail gains the provider's handle

The View action already opens a modal over the run's JSON. It gains a named block above that dump, because the one thing an operator needs from this surface is something they can paste into the provider's own dashboard: the message id. Without it the column tells you a message bounced and leaves you no way to ask why.
