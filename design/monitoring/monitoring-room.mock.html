Monitoring — a project binds its error monitor

The project-settings room a workspace connects Sentry in, binds one or more of its Sentry projects to this Motir project, sees the connection’s health, and disconnects one. Story MOTIR-4926 · Subtask MOTIR-5256; implemented by MOTIR-5262.

It COMPOSES the settings-area chrome and the repositories room’s inventory shape and re-specifies neither. What it decides is this room’s own content: the grant header, the connection rows, the health treatment, and the rail entry. Every interaction is grounded in the two cards that specify the flow — the notes name which came from which.

Panel 1 · Nothing connected — the empty state, and the door out to the provider

The room’s first state, and the one every workspace meets. One line saying what connecting DOES, because “connect Sentry” on its own does not say that issues become work items on this board — which is the whole proposition. One affordance, primary, and nothing else to decide yet: which monitored projects to bind is a choice made after the authorisation, when there is a list to choose from.

Panel 2 · Connected, one monitored project — the degenerate case of the SET

The grant is drawn ABOVE the rows, and the health belongs to it. A provider authorises at the organisation tier, so one authorisation covers every project in it (MOTIR-5258 stores the credential on the grant for exactly that reason) — which means the health verdict is a property of the connection and not of each row. Drawing a badge per row would imply three independently-broken things where there is one.

Panel 4 · Degraded — the state this epic exists to make unmissable

MOTIR-4918 measured a signal that stopped reaching anyone while every component reported success, and a credential that has quietly expired reproduces that shape one level up: the poll runs, finds nothing, and the board stays empty in a way indistinguishable from a quiet week. So this state is drawn three ways at once — a FILLED warning chip, a banner, and the two affordances that fix it — and the banner quotes Sentry’s own sentence rather than one of ours, because “the connection failed” tells a person nothing about whether to re-authorise or wait. The rows stay visible and stay accurate: they are what is bound, and the grant is what is broken.

Panel 5 · The access path — the door, drawn rather than described

Naming the route in prose is not drawing the entrance. The row below is the whole access path: Monitoring in the General group, third, directly under Repositories, with the lucide Activity mark. Grounded in PROJECT_SETTINGS_NAV’s own convention rather than invented here — the rail groups are General → Access → Work → Automation, and the reasoning for this group is in the notes.

General, not Automation. This room holds a third party’s CREDENTIAL and an inventory of what it is bound to — the same shape as Repositories, which is its named precedent and which sits in this group. The Automation group holds rooms that configure Motir’s OWN behaviour (the planner’s cadence, the rule engine). The ingestion story’s per-connection filter may well belong there, and that is its own card’s call.
value what the row shows
connecting The return leg is in flight. The row exists, is visibly not usable, and carries no action — each would be a promise the server has not made.
connected The organisation and the bound projects, with when the credential was last checked. A filled success chip on the GRANT, not per row.
degraded The provider’s own reason, verbatim, in a filled warning banner, with Re-check and Reconnect. Never a border style.
(none) The empty state, one line saying what connecting does, and one primary affordance.

What lands from this panel: one entry in PROJECT_SETTINGS_NAV, in the general group after repositories, gated on the integration:manage permission MOTIR-5260 added — so a member who cannot manage the credential does not see the door at all, which is how every other row in this rail behaves.
